CareRing Smart Health Wristband

A wearable structure and patent-oriented design project exploring health monitoring concepts, SOS support, a removable liquid cartridge and a compact ultrasonic atomization expansion module.

Overview

Design objective

The project explores how a smart wristband could accept a thin, replaceable liquid cartridge and route liquid toward a compact atomization module without turning the entire wristband into a disposable device. The work focuses on structure, assembly logic, sealing, flow path and patent-figure communication.

My Role

What I owned

  • Functional requirement decomposition
  • Expansion-module and cartridge structure planning
  • Component numbering and patent-view organization
  • Flow-path, sealing and condensation-return concepts
  • CAD modeling coordination across Shapr3D, SolidWorks and scripted tools
  • Exploded-view, section-view and patent-figure planning
  • Prototype-photo boundary review
  • Documentation and iterative design correction

The project demonstrates product structure, CAD workflow and patent-oriented communication. It does not claim completed electronics, validated atomization output, medical efficacy or production readiness.

Core Features

Proposed structure

01

Removable cartridge

A thin side-insert cartridge separates consumable liquid storage from the main body.

02

Capillary feed

A guided liquid path concept moves liquid toward the atomization area.

03

Atomization module

A compact ultrasonic component is arranged inside the detachable expansion structure.

04

Micro-pore diffusion

The outlet structure is intended to control how mist leaves the module.

05

Condensation return

A return path concept reduces uncontrolled liquid accumulation.

06

Sealing system

Sealing rings and fitted interfaces are considered around cartridge and housing contacts.

07

Independent or linked power

The concept considers both wristband-linked control and independent module power.

08

Patent-view system

Overall, exploded, section, flow-path and insertion views communicate the structure.

Limitations and Safety

Evidence boundary

Prototype images are used only to explain cartridge insertion, module placement and external fit. They do not demonstrate electronics, liquid safety, output-particle performance, health-monitoring accuracy or a finished medical device.
